CoatSim — model notes

CoatSim solves the thin-film (lubrication) equations on the coated surface of a catheter, tube, IOL cartridge or flat coupon. The film is assumed thin compared with the part's radius and gently sloped, which is the standard model for coating flows (drainage, levelling, wetting, sag and beading).

  • Capillarity: surface tension × surface curvature, including the cross-section curvature that drives Rayleigh–Plateau beading on wires and collar/plug formation in lumens.
  • Wetting: equilibrium contact angle from Owens–Wendt surface-energy components (substrate vs coating), with Wenzel roughness and plasma activation/hydrophobic recovery. Enforced with a disjoining-pressure precursor-film model.
  • Gravity: drainage along and around the part for any tilt and rotation speed.
  • Airflow: interfacial shear and enhanced evaporation from air knives, HACS-style lumen airflow and vacuum air draw.
  • Film formation: solvent evaporation (temperature and airflow dependent, slower deep inside lumens), solids build-up raising viscosity, and thermal (Arrhenius) or UV cure kinetics leading to gelation.
  • Deposition: dip coating (Landau–Levich/fibre laws), vacuum withdraw and lumen draining (Bretherton/Aussillous–Quéré films behind moving menisci with Poiseuille plug dynamics and capillary pressure), spray (Gaussian footprint, incidence, rotation) and drip/needle insertion.

Limits. Contact lines are resolved only down to the grid size; very thick puddles (comparable to the lumen radius) are approximate; material values are typical literature values. Calibrate the coating properties against a measured film before relying on absolute thicknesses. The tool is meant to compare options and explain trends quickly.

Controls. 3D view: drag to rotate, right-drag or shift-drag to pan, wheel to zoom. Ctrl+Enter runs, Space plays/pauses the timeline.
